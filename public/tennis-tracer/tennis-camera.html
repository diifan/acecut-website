Tennis camera: how to choose one and place it for match video

Choose a tennis camera by what the footage must show: both baselines and sidelines for reviewing points, a clear view of the ball against the background, and a mount that stays fixed during play. Test any option on your own court before relying on it.

Decide what the recording must show

Pick the question first. Reviewing whole points needs the full court and both players in one frame; reviewing a single stroke needs a closer view of the swing and the ball. One wide view rarely serves both well, so choose the main purpose before comparing cameras.

Run a short test on your own court

Record one short rally at the court and time of day you normally play. Check whether the ball stays visible at the far baseline, whether glare or shadow hides it, whether the lines you care about are inside the frame, and whether the picture stays steady if the mount is nudged by wind or a moving fence.

Fix the position and keep it

Keep one camera position for a session and write it down, because moving it changes the perspective and makes clips hard to compare. If you plan to attach anything to a fence or post, ask the venue first.

What the cited vendor page covers

The reference is a vendor page for the Spintip app. For that app it describes putting one phone on a tripod or the fence, behind the baseline. That is the vendor’s setup for its own product, not an AceCut requirement, and this page does not vouch for any feature of that app.

Use this checklist

  • Write down whether the footage must show whole points or one stroke.
  • Record a short test rally on your own court.
  • Check ball visibility at the far baseline and in glare or shadow.
  • Confirm the mount stays fixed and that you may use it at the venue.
  • Keep the original clip unedited.
